* Findings that change the design

- For thread history the generation does not determine what resumes; the rollout does: the server rebuilds the SQLite history from the rollout at the absolute path in =threads.path=, fails every history read without it, and overrides a restored database with whatever the file holds (12 of 20 warm restores showed more turns than their capture).
  The design must either bring the rollout into the generation, which needs =sessions/= to be relocatable and on this build it is not, or drop the history-consistency guarantee and document it as "whatever the shared rollout contains"; the record names the choice without making it.
- Unattended =thread/resume= executes a queued message once and, with an =active= goal, issues about twenty model requests per second until stopped; =paused= is a usable hold for goals and the queue has none.
  The runner must check =goals_1= and =queue_1= before resuming unattended.
- Two servers with different homes on one =sqlite_home= are two writers with no refusal from Codex; lineage exclusivity stays the runner's job.
- Median turn 0.51 s with everything on Lustre, 0.21 s with the databases local, 0.042 s with the home local as well: the rollout traffic is most of what remains after the databases move.
- 20/20 seeded abrupt-stop interleavings restored every completed turn from warm and post-crash captures; cross-database skew at a crash remains unmeasured because =state_5= holds only the preview.
- From the inventory phase: all six databases follow =sqlite_home= (=-c= over =config.toml= over =CODEX_SQLITE_HOME=); locks, =sessions/= and =history.jsonl= stay in =CODEX_HOME=; no silent fallback; stdin EOF leaves data in WAL.
