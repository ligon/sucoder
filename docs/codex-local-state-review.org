* Storage and recovery findings

** P1: historical sealing did not fence later writers

A sealed manifest showed that capture happened after shutdown, but did not prevent the predecessor from restarting or two successors from restoring the same history.
Lost acknowledgements could incorrectly be treated as valid transfers merely because the generation existed.

Disposition: accepted.
[[file:codex-local-state.org::*Transfer fencing and parent pins][Transfer fencing]] now requires durable offered/claimed/revoked records, one short metadata critical section, nonce/generation/hash binding and revocation before reopening predecessor writers.
Ordinary clean-exit checkpoints follow the same rule.
Unresolved successor startup blocks predecessor restart.

** P1: process exit did not prove application consistency

A crash between related commits in different databases could leave a quiescent but inconsistent set, which the draft could label sealed.

Disposition: accepted.
[[file:codex-local-state.org::*Two consistency classes][Consistency classes]] now require profile-verified successful graceful shutdown/quiescence for sealed status.
Abnormal, forced or unknown exits remain crash candidates subject to application recovery gates, including in-place restart policy.
The spike must interrupt cross-store updates and verify that later capture cannot silently upgrade that state.

** P1: restored forks could share upstream thread identities

A new SuCoder run UUID did not change copied Codex thread IDs or embedded external transcript paths.
Two local databases could still write the same shared assets.

Disposition: accepted.
Concurrent restored forks are deferred until upstream identity and external-file isolation is established.
Independent fresh histories remain supported.

** P1: parent preservation raced retention

A child could select a parent between the publisher's reference check and deletion, leaving a missing recovery generation.

Disposition: accepted.
Parent validation, durable pin creation and child provenance publication share the metadata critical section with retention's retiring transition.
Database copying and file deletion happen outside that section.
Failed child setup leaves a conservative pin until positively reconciled.

** P2: diagnostic-log classification contradicted itself

The inventory table excluded logs immediately while a later clause required evidence that they were disposable.

Disposition: accepted.
The inventory now preserves logs until the storage profile proves they can be omitted.

** Confirmation refinements

The storage reviewer confirmed the five original findings were addressed, then identified two narrower ambiguities.
Both were accepted: claims enforce exclusivity over a complete lineage even when they select different generations, and an ordinary-exit offer may leave its successor unbound until a single serialized claim binds it.
Dedicated race cases are included in the validation plan.

* Lifecycle findings

** P1: confined renewal could adopt and cancel its predecessor

=_relaunch_session= clears YAML allocation fields at =sucoder/cli.py:3421=, but =_launch_confined= rediscovers the still-live same-target job at =sucoder/mirror.py:3058=.
The generic loop could then receive the old job ID as the replacement and cancel it at =sucoder/renew.py:253=.

Disposition: accepted as a required integration change, not repaired in this documentation change.
[[file:codex-local-state.org::*Planned renewal sequence][Renewal]] now carries explicit replacement authorization excluding only the verified drained predecessor, retains normal duplicate-job protection, and requires a different new allocation identity.
The plan includes a regression that drives the real confined adoption branch.

** P1: failed replacement lost predecessor provenance

The current relaunch path clears old allocation metadata before attempting the replacement.
An exception after submission can also leave a pending new job that must not be submitted again.

Disposition: accepted.
The design retains old and pending-new identities in a durable attempt record and requires typed pending/ready/failed/unknown outcomes and exact-attempt reconciliation.

** P1: a published generation did not prove current handoff validity

This independently confirmed the storage review's fencing finding.
Delayed or lost acknowledgements must be reconciled against current transfer authority as well as the immutable generation.

Disposition: accepted through the shared fencing protocol.

** P1: tmux existence was insufficient replacement readiness

The confined readiness check at =sucoder/mirror.py:3233= observes tmux, but its surviving shell can remain after Codex or the state runner fails.

Disposition: accepted.
[[file:codex-local-state.org::*Startup and placement][Startup]] requires a managed runtime handshake naming scope/run, exact parent/hash, allocation, effective SQLite placement and independently owned foreground server.
Renewal rechecks it before predecessor cancellation.

** P2: managed TUI launch selection was not preserved

=RemoteSession.service_launch= validates only remote-control commands; an ordinary CLI-selected Codex TUI could renew into a different configured default harness.

Disposition: accepted.
The design adds a validated managed-owner descriptor retaining original Codex command/model for both supported modes, while preserving nonmanaged renewal behavior.
The test plan includes configured Claude with a CLI-selected managed Codex TUI.

** Sibling detach

The reviewer requested an explicit save for the departing owner on sibling-detach release.
The revised design drains only that owner before stopping its tmux session, preserving sibling owners and their allocation.
Client-only detachment leaves the owner active and cannot create a sealed checkpoint.

* Verdict and remaining gates

The lifecycle confirmation found all five findings and the sibling-detach note addressed, with no remaining blocking written lifecycle contradiction.
The storage confirmation found its original findings addressed and judged the design ready for a disposable capability spike; its two final clarifications were incorporated and explicitly confirmed in a final pass.

The design is ready to guide that isolated spike.
Runtime implementation and production opt-in remain conditional on evidence for:

- Complete SQLite and maintenance-lock redirection on the actual Savio Codex build.
- Independent foreground-server ownership in both supported launch modes.
- Successful graceful shutdown and portable conversation resume after cwd/allocation changes.
- Safe queue/goal replay control and the absence of duplicated actions.
- Application-level warm/crash recovery across all required stores, or continued sealed-only automatic restore.
- Lustre publication/metadata primitives and measured checkpoint cost.

No claim of a ten-minute guaranteed recovery window or complete elimination of shared-home I/O is made.
No runtime source, live cluster state or user history was changed by this design review.
